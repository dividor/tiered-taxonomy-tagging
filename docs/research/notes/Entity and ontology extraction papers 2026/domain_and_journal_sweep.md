# Domain and journal sweep: entity extraction, knowledge graphs and ontologies for documents like UN evaluation reports (window May to November 2026)

How to read the entries. Today is 2026-11-05. Each paper lists first-posted and published dates separately, then a `Read:` tag saying exactly what was read.

- `Read: full text` = the whole paper was fetched. Numbers marked this way were transcribed by a summarising fetch tool or by my own text extraction, so check them against the paper before quoting.
- `Read: part of full text` = only the named parts were read.
- `Read: abstract only` = the abstract came from Crossref, OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar or the arXiv abstract page. The paper body was not read.
- `Read: metadata only (NOT READ)` = title, authors, venue and dates only. No abstract was available to me. These are listed so you can obtain them. Nothing is claimed about their content beyond the title.

Dates. "Crossref created" is the date the DOI record was registered. For Elsevier papers it is my only proxy for the online-first date, because ScienceDirect returned HTTP 403 and Crossref carries no separate online date for them. Identifiers were checked against Crossref (DOIs) or the arXiv abstract page (arXiv IDs) unless an entry says otherwise. Metadata and abstracts for entries were pulled by script from those sources.

Tracks. "Discovery" means discovering an ontology from text. "Mapping" means mapping text to a supplied ontology.

## Has anyone built an ontology or knowledge graph from evaluation reports, development or humanitarian documents, or similar institutional reports in this window? What did they find?

### Takeaway
I found no paper in the window that builds an ontology or knowledge graph from evaluation reports specifically. The nearest work is UNDP's AIDA platform (7,000+ evaluation reports, taxonomy tags in version 1, vector retrieval in version 2, no graph reported in the part I read) and humanitarian graph pipelines over ReliefWeb and PreventionWeb, which use free-form causal graphs or fixed extraction schemas. Across these papers, fixed schemas and controlled vocabularies are common and bottom-up ontology discovery from this kind of corpus is rare.

- **AIDA: Assessment of development analytics** (book chapter). Authors: Richard Jones, Davor Djosan, Anish Lal Pradhan. First posted: none found. Published: 2026-08-18 (Crossref). Venue: *From Algorithms to Evidence* (Routledge, open access). [DOI 10.4324/9781003799139-20](https://doi.org/10.4324/9781003799139-20). `Read: part of full text` (main body, CC BY-NC-ND chapter PDF).
  - Method: Phase 1 (2020 to 2023) used custom parsers (Apache Tika, Textract), a thematic taxonomy and classifiers trained on 500 hand-labelled evaluations. Phase 2 (2025) used Azure Document Intelligence, chunking, embeddings, retrieval with filters, and GPT responses with citations.
  - Evidence: over 7,000 UNDP evaluation reports spanning more than 25 years, averaging about 70 pages. Under 1% could not be ingested. The classifier for findings, conclusions and recommendations plus taxonomy tags had F1 0.85. Roughly 20% of the corpus is French or Spanish. Operating cost fell about 70% in version 2. The chapter says OCR limits on scanned documents can drop context or introduce minor inaccuracies, and that treating each paragraph as a separate document in version 1 gave fragmented insights. In the part read it reports no knowledge graph or ontology, and no formal retrieval or extraction metrics for version 2.
  - Relevance: the same document type at about half your scale. Mapping track with a supplied taxonomy. Note its F1 is for the older classifier, not version 2.
- **Governing the evidence base: An empirical framework of AI archetypes for policy analysis.** Authors: Aleksei Turobov, Diane Coyle. First posted: none found. Published online: 2026-09-24. Venue: *Data & Policy* 8, e47 (CC BY). [DOI 10.1017/dap.2026.10100](https://doi.org/10.1017/dap.2026.10100). `Read: full text` (via fetch tool).

**Humanitarian and disaster graphs and extraction**

- **Verifiable Disaster Storylines and Causal Knowledge Graphs: A Citation-Grounded Pipeline from Heterogeneous Humanitarian Sources.** Authors: Ivan Decostanzi, Michele Ronco, Sergio Consoli, Christina Corbane, Lorenzo Bertolini, Indaco Biazzo, Daria Mihaila, Manuel Garcia-Herranz, Felix Schwebel, Yelena Mejova, Kyriaki Kalimeri. First posted: 2026-09-01 (v2 2026-09-13). Published: GoodIT '26 (issue date 2026-09-02; online 2026-10-06). [arXiv 2609.00858](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00858), [DOI 10.1145/3794786.3830759](https://doi.org/10.1145/3794786.3830759). `Read: full text` (via fetch tool).
  - Method: EM-DAT, ReliefWeb and European Media Monitor, linked by GLIDE IDs. Four-sentence chunks with one sentence overlap, bge-m3 retrieval, 17-field storylines, then causal triples limited to "causes" and "prevents". No ontology or node-type schema is described; node labels are free form.
  - Evidence: 3 events; 18 annotators. Retrieval relevance 85.8%, informativeness 72.0%. KG triple faithfulness 86.7% supported (fully 56.1%, partially 30.6%). KG node relevance 94.0%, link relevance 92.3%. Trust 6.56 out of 10 from experts. Experts rated causal graphs as the least useful feature (2.78 of 5) and KG citations as the most useful (4.33 of 5). Inter-annotator chance-corrected agreement was low (Krippendorff's alpha roughly 0.1 to 0.5). Languages, PDF noise and table parsing are not discussed, though "garbled text" appears as a category for non-informative retrieved paragraphs.
  - Relevance: discovery-style free-form graphs. The low expert rating of graphs compared with citations is a useful caution.
- **From UNDRR Reports to Event Records: Schema-Constrained LLM Extraction of Georeferenced Disasters.** Authors: Camilla Andreozzi, Phuong-Anh Nguyen-Le, Zhijing Jin, Revati Mani. First posted: 2026-09-20. Published: not found. [arXiv 2609.23853](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23853). `Read: full text` (via fetch tool; headline numbers confirmed against the arXiv abstract).
  - Method: fixed schema and controlled hazard vocabulary with structured outputs on the first 12,000 characters of each document; no multi-window chunking, so tables and annexes count only if they fall in that window.
  - Evidence: 10,000 PreventionWeb documents (98.9% English; 52 Spanish, 27 Arabic, 16 Japanese, 14 French; authors say too skewed for language comparison). 3,572 records from 1,913 documents, 24 hazard types, 81% of locations resolved. On 171 positive documents: GPT-5 pooled attribute F1 86.0%, GPT-5.4 86.6%, spaCy-gazetteer baseline 44.2%. Complete-match accuracy only 44.4% for GPT-5 and 38.0% for GPT-5.4. Exact location F1 66.3% against fuzzy 80.3% (GPT-5). Verbatim evidence located 72.0% for GPT-5, 47.2% for GPT-5.4, up to 94.6% for DeepSeek V3.1. Event-argument assignment is not assessed.
  - Relevance: mapping track at real scale on UN-hosted grey literature, and the most useful failure list here: date free-text in 428 forms, publication-date substitution, role confusion between hazards' features and affected places.
- **Causal Evidence Extraction and Triangulation in Crisis Reports using Large Language Models: A ReliefWeb-based Study.** Authors: Yuanjun Zhang, Mourad Oussalah. First posted: 2026-08-05. Published: July 2026, Findings of ACL 2026, pp. 32478-32491. [DOI 10.18653/v1/2026.findings-acl.1626](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2026.findings-acl.1626), [arXiv 2608.04576](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04576). `Read: abstract only`.
  - Method: two-stage extraction of intervention-outcome records with direction and strength, query-conditioned, with snippet grounding, then a Level-of-Evidence score.
  - Evidence: 100 expert-annotated reports; weighted F1 90.73% (best closed model), 94.15% (fine-tuned Llama-3.1-8B); cash-assistance food outcomes LoE 0.865.
  - Relevance: the most directly reusable extraction target for evaluation reports (intervention, outcome, direction, strength). Abstract describes humanitarian reports as "long, noisy, and multi-topic".
- **A large-language-model framework for automated humanitarian situation reporting.** Authors: Ivan Decostanzi, Yelena Mejova, Kyriaki Kalimeri. Received: 2025-12-03; Accepted: 2026-08-19; Published: 2026-08-28 (early version). *Scientific Reports*. [DOI 10.1038/s41598-026-68010-x](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-68010-x). `Read: abstract and landing page` (the page carries an early-release notice; body not read).

### Inferences
- Inference: the evaluation field has adopted retrieval and supplied taxonomies but has not published ontology or graph construction. Absence in my sweep does not prove absence in practice, because agency work may sit in reports and blogs I did not fully sweep.
- Inference: a dual track looks defensible from this evidence. Discovery work appears strongest when constrained to a small extraction target (intervention, outcome, direction, strength; hazard, place, date) and weakest when graphs are free form, as the expert usefulness ratings in the disaster storyline paper hint. This comes from a small sample and different tasks.
- Inference: the recurring over-labelling on SDG and STIP mapping suggests per-label calibration will be needed for mapping to a supplied ontology.
- Hypothesis, not tested: AIDA's chunking experience (fixed length to semantic with overlap, then structure-aware) may be directly reusable, as your corpus already has chunks.

### Gaps
- No paper found that reports an ontology built from UN evaluation reports. No paper found using UNEG, 3ie, IEG or OECD DAC evaluation repositories for knowledge graph or ontology work.
- AIDA reports no measured retrieval or extraction accuracy for version 2 in the portion I read. I did not read its last pages.
- Audit: no knowledge graph or ontology paper on public audit reports was found.
- Whether the CGIAR, results-measurement and IEG chapters report further numbers: I read only excerpts of them.

## How do extraction and ontology methods hold up on noisy PDF text, tables, long documents and non-English text, with numbers?

### Takeaway
Measured degradation is real but rarely measured on institutional reports. The best numbers come from adjacent domains: a raw open-weight model fell to 0.216 partial-mention F1 on historical OCR, and the same model reached about 0.78 entity-level F1 with a structured pipeline (the abstract reports these on different measures). Removing table reconstruction cut exact match from 0.96 to 0.75 in sustainability reports. Non-English document-level ontology extraction evidence is thin. The main ontology learning shared task runs on synthetic clean text, so its strong scores cannot be carried over to your corpus.

### Inferences
- Inference: LLMs4OL's Reuse (0.98) against Flagship (0.78) gap supports starting from a seed vocabulary, but both numbers are on synthetic clean short text. - Inference: expect relation extraction to be much weaker than entity extraction. Ilves, RE-ErrorTax, LLMs4OL's non-taxonomic results and the French study all point in the same direction.
- Inference: evidence for parsing effects suggests measuring your parse noise as its own variable rather than assuming a clean-text benchmark carries over, per Fouss and Anvari. Their domains differ from yours.
- Hypothesis, not tested: Scope3Trace's table result may overstate gains for your corpus, since its targets are narrow numeric fields that live mainly in tables.
